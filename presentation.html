Context & Motivation

The Core Problem: Preventing Costly Machine Failures

Moving from reactive and scheduled maintenance to proactive, condition-based intelligence

⚠️ Flaws of Conventional Maintenance
  • Reactive Maintenance (Run-to-Failure): Machinery is operated until it breaks. In mission-critical aerospace and industrial turbines, unexpected failure causes catastrophic safety risks, flight disruptions, and millions in damages.
  • Preventive Maintenance (Time-Based): Parts are replaced on fixed calendar or hour intervals. This results in discarding perfectly healthy components with 30-40% useful life remaining, driving up operational expenses.
  • Lack of Real-Time Visibility: Static maintenance schedules ignore true internal wear, ambient operational stresses, and progressive mechanical fatigue.
💡 The Predictive Maintenance (PdM) Solution
  • Condition-Based Continuous Monitoring: Ingests live telemetry from internal temperature, pressure, and vibration sensors to track internal component degradation in real time.
  • Remaining Useful Life (RUL) Target: Accurately predicts exactly how many operational cycles are left before a component exceeds safety margins (RUL = Cycle_failure - Cycle_current).
  • Just-In-Time Servicing: Maintenance is scheduled precisely when needed—maximizing component lifespan while maintaining zero tolerance for catastrophic failures.
  • Fulfills Course Mandate: Directly tackles multi-sensor device acquisition, data pipeline hygiene, and multi-model predictive prognostics.
Device Telemetry Acquisition

Device Under Study: Turbofan Jet Engine & NASA Telemetry

Comprehensive multi-channel thermodynamics from healthy operation through failure

✈️ Turbofan Engine Subsystems
  • Fan & Low-Pressure Compressor (LPC): First air intake and bypass compression; tracked by inlet temperature (T2) and fan speed (Nf).
  • High-Pressure Compressor (HPC): Generates high combustion pressure; blade degradation causes surges in outlet temperature (T30) and static pressure (P30).
  • Combustion Chamber & High-Pressure Turbine (HPT): Extreme thermal stress zones; monitored via coolant bleed enthalpy and turbine exit temperature (T50).
  • Low-Pressure Turbine (LPT) & Exhaust Nozzle: Expands hot core gas to drive the fan spool; monitored via bypass ratio and pressure (P15).
📊 Dataset Architecture (NASA C-MAPSS FD001)
  • Gold-Standard Benchmark: Released by NASA Ames Research Center for Prognostics and Health Management (PHM).
  • Fleet Size: 100 Run-to-Failure training engines (20,631 total cycle recordings) and 100 test engines with ground truth validation RUL.
  • Telemetry Stream: 3 operational setting dials (altitude, Mach number, throttle resolver) + 21 high-precision physical/virtual sensor streams.
  • Standard Operating Condition: Sea level flight condition with single fault mode (HPC degradation), enabling clear evaluation of prognostic algorithms.
Data Engineering Pipeline

Feature Engineering & Strict Leakage Prevention

Transforming raw thermodynamic readings into stable, trend-aware prognostic features

1. Dead Sensor Pruning & Target Formulation
  • Constant Sensor Pruning: Sensors 1, 5, 6, 10, 16, 18, and 19 exhibit zero variance across all cycles under FD001 conditions. Dropped to eliminate noisy dead weights.
  • Piecewise Linear RUL Target: In early life, engines operate in an un-degraded healthy plateau. We cap RUL at 125 cycles early on, transitioning into linear degradation. This prevents models from guessing unrealistic early-life numbers.
2. Rolling Temporal Features & Leakage Control
  • Rolling Mean (5-cycle window): Filters high-frequency measurement jitter while capturing the underlying monotonic degradation momentum.
  • Rolling Std (5-cycle window): Captures mechanical instability. High sensor variance strongly correlates with imminent blade tip rubs and seal degradation.
  • Zero Data Leakage: StandardScaler is fitted strictly on the training partition only. Test engine data is only transformed using frozen train statistics.
Modeling Methodology

Progressive Architecture Progression: Baseline to Deep Learning

Evaluating baseline regression, non-linear ensembles, and temporal recurrent networks

1. Linear Regression
  • Role: Empirical baseline.
  • Mechanism: Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) fitting continuous linear weights.
  • Weakness: Fails to model non-linear accelerated wear curves near end of life.
2. Random Forest
  • Role: Bagging ensemble.
  • Mechanism: 100 decision trees with feature subsampling.
  • Strength: Robust against outliers and captures non-linear decision thresholds.
3. XGBoost
  • Role: Gradient boosted trees.
  • Mechanism: Sequential residual error minimization with depth regularization.
  • Strength: Highest performance among tabular non-sequential models.
4. PyTorch LSTM
  • Role: Sequential Deep Learning.
  • Mechanism: 2-layer Recurrent LSTM (hidden_dim=64, Dropout=0.2).
  • Superpower: Maintains memory state over a 30-cycle sliding window.
Empirical Results

Model Performance Benchmark on NASA Test Fleet

Temporal Deep Learning (LSTM) outperforms standard tabular models across all metrics

📈 Benchmark Results (Test Fleet: 100 Units)
Model Architecture MAE ↓ RMSE ↓ R² Score ↑
Linear Regression (Baseline) 26.78 33.36 0.3556
Random Forest Regressor 23.47 33.80 0.3385
Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) 24.65 33.06 0.3670
PyTorch LSTM (Deep Learning) 21.63 31.19 0.4365
  • 19.2% MAE Improvement: LSTM reduced average prediction error from 26.78 cycles (Linear) to 21.63 cycles.
  • Outlier Penalty (RMSE 31.19): LSTM penalizes large errors heavily, preventing critical late-failure mispredictions.
  • Higher Explained Variance (R² = 0.4365): Demonstrates the indispensable value of temporal sliding window memory.
Exploratory Data Analysis

Physicochemical Degradation Signatures in Telemetry

Tracking thermodynamic deviations as internal engine components wear out

🔍 Physical Degradation Insights
  • Monotonic Thermal Drift (T30, T50): High-Pressure Compressor outlet temperature and turbine exit temperature rise steadily due to blade tip clearance erosion and combustion friction.
  • Spool Speed Degradation (Nf, Nc): Fan and core rotational speeds drop under identical fuel flows due to aerodynamic efficiency losses in the compressor stages.
  • Pressure Drops (P30, P15): Total pressure at the compressor discharge port degrades progressively over time.
  • Clear Prognostic Window: Measurable degradation becomes distinctly noticeable 50 to 70 cycles prior to failure, providing a viable operational window for scheduled maintenance.

Academic Defense & Viva Prep

Technical Justifications & Viva Cheat-Sheet

Answers to fundamental machine learning, prognostics, and architectural questions

Theoretical Justifications
  • Why Regression over Classification? Binary classification ("Good" vs "Bad") provides no runway to schedule hangar crews or order parts. Regression gives the exact cycle countdown.
  • Why MAE/RMSE instead of Accuracy? Accuracy is invalid for continuous regression targets. MAE measures mean cycle deviation; RMSE heavily penalizes dangerous large errors.
  • How was Overfitting controlled? PyTorch Dropout (0.2), early stopping on validation loss, and 3-fold cross-validation with shallow tree depth in ensembles.
Pipeline Integrity & Limitations
  • How was Data Leakage eliminated? Normalization parameters (mean, std) were fitted strictly on the training engines only. Test data was never used during fit.
  • Why does LSTM beat Random Forest? Mechanical wear is non-Markovian: cycle t depends on cumulative mechanical fatigue over past cycles. LSTMs retain memory over sliding windows.
  • Project Limitations: FD001 assumes Sea Level flight conditions. Real operations involve varying altitudes and multi-fault modes (as modeled in C-MAPSS FD002-FD004).
